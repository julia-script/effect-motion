---
title: Depth of field
description: Give the camera a lens — a sharp focus plane, and blur that grows with depth
---

The default camera is a pinhole: everything is sharp at every depth. Give it
an `aperture` and it becomes a lens. One plane in front of it stays sharp, and
everything nearer or farther blurs, more the farther it sits from that plane.
Both settings are ordinary numeric camera fields, so the animators you already
use drive them, and a rack focus is just a tween.

## The two fields

- **`focusDistance`**: how far in front of the lens the sharp plane sits,
  measured along the view. It defaults to the camera's resting distance, so
  for an untouched camera the `z: 0` plane is in focus.
- **`aperture`**: the lens radius, in world units. `0` (the default) is a
  pinhole, and the renderer skips depth of field entirely. Bigger values give
  a shallower depth of field: less of the scene stays sharp.

Set them like any other field:

```ts
const camera = yield* Scene.camera;
yield* Scene.update(camera, (props) => ({ ...props, aperture: 40 }));
```

## How much blur

The blur follows the thin-lens model. A point at view distance `d` blurs to a
disc of radius

```
aperture · |d − focusDistance| / d
```

in world units on the focus plane. At the camera's resting distance one world
unit on the focus plane is one pixel, so for a resting camera this is the blur
radius in pixels.

As an example, take a 1920-wide scene. Its camera rests about 2667 units back,
so the focus plane is at `z: 0`. With `aperture: 40`, a shape at `z: -1000`
sits at `d ≈ 3667` and blurs by about `40 · 1000 / 3667 ≈ 11` px. A shape
1000 units *in front* of the plane (`d ≈ 1667`) blurs by about 24 px: blur
grows faster in front of the focus plane than behind it, just like a real
lens. The renderer caps the blur radius at 30 rendered pixels, which is 15
px on screen at a pixel ratio of 2.

Blur is worked out per pixel from depth, not per shape. A plane tilted away
from the camera is sharp along one band and softens smoothly on either side,
with no steps. See the [tilted plane](/docs/examples/tilted-plane) example.

## Focusing on something

`focusDistance` is a distance from the lens, not a `z` coordinate. For a
camera that isn't rotated, focusing on a layer at depth `z` means
`focusDistance = camera.z - z`:

```ts
const { position } = yield* Scene.data(camera);
const focusOn = (z: number) => ({ focusDistance: position.z - z });
```

For a camera aimed with `Camera.lookAt`, the distance to the point of interest
is the one that keeps the subject sharp. `Camera.orbitTo` keeps that distance
the same, so the focus holds while the camera orbits. `Camera.dollyTo` changes
it, so pull focus along with the dolly, using the same duration and easing:

```ts
yield* Scene.all([
	Camera.dollyTo(camera, 1900, "2 seconds", "easeInOutCubic"),
	Motion.tweenTo(camera, { focusDistance: 1900 }, "2 seconds", "easeInOutCubic"),
]);
```

## Rack focus

A rack focus moves the sharp plane from one subject to another while the
camera holds still. That's a `focusDistance` tween:

```ts
yield* camera.pipe(
	Motion.tweenTo(focusOn(FRONT), "1200 millis", "easeInOutCubic"),
	Motion.wait("700 millis"),
	Motion.tweenTo(focusOn(BACK), "1200 millis", "easeInOutCubic"),
);
```

## The HUD stays sharp

`Hud` content is drawn after, and on top of, the depth-of-field pass. Titles
and labels on the HUD stay crisp whatever the camera's aperture.

## Limits

- **Opaque shapes only.** Blur comes from the depth buffer, which holds one
  depth per pixel. A semi-transparent shape blurs at its own depth, not at the
  depth of whatever shows through it. Keep shapes in depth-of-field scenes at
  `opacity: 1`.
- **Perspective cameras only.**
- **Cost.** With `aperture > 0` the scene is drawn twice per frame and every
  pixel gathers dozens of blur samples. The sampling cost grows with the pixel
  count; the second draw costs CPU time per shape, so scenes with hundreds of
  shapes can drop frames in the browser. At `aperture: 0` there is no extra
  cost.
- **Real time vs export.** The browser renderer (and so the `Player`) takes
  half the blur samples by default, which halves the blur's GPU cost; the blur
  shape is the same, with a little more fine grain. Video export always uses
  full quality. For full quality in the browser, for example when recording
  the canvas, pass `dofQuality: "full"` to `Renderer.make`.
- **Outlines.** Shapes default to a 1 px black stroke. A layer close to the
  lens scales that up into a visible dark outline, so flat-colour scenes
  usually set `strokeWidth: 0`.

For a camera orbiting a sharp subject through a field of blurred shapes, see
[focus orbit](/docs/examples/focus-orbit).
