---
title: Rack focus
description: The sharp plane slides from foreground to background while the camera holds still
---

**The idea:** a rack focus is a tween. Three layers of flat shapes sit at
three depths and the camera never moves; the only thing animating is the
camera's `focusDistance`. As it travels, the sharp plane slides from the
shapes crowding the lens, to the subject, to the wall far behind — the same
pull a camera operator makes by turning the focus ring.

`focusDistance` is a *view* distance (how far in front of the lens the sharp
plane sits), so each layer's focus is the camera's resting `z` minus the
layer's `z`. `aperture` is the lens radius in world units: bigger means a
shallower depth of field.
