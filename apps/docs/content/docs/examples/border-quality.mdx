A reproducible scene collection for investigating border quality. Each sample
uses the current renderer. Pause and scrub the players to compare specific frames;
the code below each player exposes all dimensions and paint settings.

## Widths and shapes

Thirty stationary samples: six stroke widths (**0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16** scene
units) across rectangles, circles, ellipses, closed paths and diagonal lines.
Every sample has a transparent fill and the same opaque stroke color.

<Example name="border-quality" />

- Compare **Rect** with **Closed Path**: both describe the same 110 × 74 outline.
- Inspect corners for gaps or bulges, and curves for visible straight segments.
- The closed path starts at its **top-left corner**: inspect that closure seam.
- Compare thin horizontal, vertical and diagonal edges for uneven coverage.

## Joins, transparency and motion

Six cases isolate conditions that can make artifacts easier to see. The top row
stays still; each animated sample in the bottom row has a stationary reference.

<Example name="border-stress" />

| Case | What to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Alpha and fill | A 24-unit border over an opaque fill, at stroke alpha 1 and 0.5. Look for darker or brighter joins and fill/stroke seams. |
| Acute joins and closure | Closed triangles on a light background, with a 20-unit stroke at alpha 1 and 0.5. The closure is at the top tip. |
| Small radii | Circles of radius 4, 12 and 40 with the same 8-unit stroke. Inspect the hole, silhouette and curved edges. |
| Subpixel motion | A 1-unit border translates by exactly 1 scene unit on both axes over four seconds. Look for shimmer and sudden coverage changes. |
| Rotation | A 4-unit rectangle border rotates from 0° to 45°. Compare edge thickness and corners with the fixed reference. |
| Perspective tilt | An 8-unit rectangle border tilts from 0° to 60° around Y. Compare near/far edges and joins. |

**Timeline:** hold for 0–2 seconds, animate during 2–6 seconds, then hold until
8 seconds. Scrub to 2, 4 and 6 seconds for the initial, midpoint and final states.

## Comparing observations

The embedded players scale to the page. A scene unit is not necessarily a screen
pixel: browser zoom, device pixel ratio and player size all affect apparent
sharpness. Compare at the same player size and zoom. For closer inspection,
increase browser zoom or render the scene at its declared resolution. Judge visual
quality rather than byte-for-byte pixel equality.

When recording a finding, include the case, width, timestamp, browser, zoom and
display scale. This separates stable join/geometry defects from changes caused by
display scaling and antialiasing.

## Paint order and embedded surfaces

The width and stress scenes use the default paint mode: all backgrounds and
outlines share z=0, and later children paint on top. No background depth offsets
are needed.

<Example name="paint-order" />

This depth scene contains explicit paint groups. At 2 seconds the left panel
swaps its overlapping circles without recreating them. The center panel applies
opacity once to the completed group. The right panel tilts the whole surface.
Below, an outlined group preserves its transparent hole, and two depth controls
keep the nearer orange object in front despite reversed authoring order.

Use `Scene.make(generator, { compositing: "depth" })` for physical world
occlusion. Use `Scene.instantiate("Group", { compositing: "paint", children })`
for a flat panel inside it. Plain groups inherit the enclosing mode; paint groups
use content bounds and apply their position, rotation, scale and opacity once.
Nested `Scene.play` compositions retain the child scene's declared mode and bounds.

## One opacity application per stroke

<Example name="stroke-union" />

Each column uses one stroke alpha. Joins, closure, self-crossings, retracing and
multiple subpaths of the same Path contribute to a single painted region. The
last row deliberately uses two separate Line entities, so its crossing blends
twice. The rectangle's inner and outer border blend over different backgrounds.

This applies to Rect, Circle, Ellipse and constant-local-z Line/Path strokes,
including planar content tilted in a depth scene. Nonplanar 3D lines retain their
per-point depth behavior. Color and opacity fades reuse the retained coverage.

## Fractional coverage and output scale

<Example name="subpixel-strokes" />

Widths **0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5 and 2** cross offsets **0, 0.25, 0.5 and
0.75** on both axes. Each cell contains horizontal/vertical rectangle edges, a
diagonal and a circle. Width zero is intentionally empty. After one second, the
matrix moves by exactly (1, 1) over four seconds while the lower rectangle's width
changes from 0.25 to 1.5. Inspect 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 seconds.

At native 1920×1080 output and DPR 1, scene units equal device pixels. At DPR 2,
each scene unit spans two device pixels. The Player uses native display DPR, but
its CSS size still scales the finished image to fit this page. A faint subpixel
line may become much fainter when a 1920-wide image is reduced to a narrow player;
compare exported native images before diagnosing coordinate rounding.

The same fixture is available as `subpixel-grouped` (paint groups in a depth
scene) and `subpixel-depth` (direct planar depth strokes). Their authored geometry
and projected size match the root paint case. Edge softness may change with pixel
phase; complete edges should remain continuous. Coverage masks are supersampled
and filtered before compositing, with the same multisampling policy on paint
surfaces and the final scene render.
