Every entity has a `position` with a **z**. Flat shapes placed at different
depths are just layers — until the camera moves, and parallax, perspective
scale, and focus turn them into a 2.5D set. This is the After Effects camera
model, in code.

## The default camera

Every scene has one, at rest: positioned back along +z so that at `z: 0` one
scene unit is one pixel, with a 50mm-equivalent focal length. You never have
to touch it — every scene so far in this path used it unknowingly.

Get a handle with `Scene.camera`, or instantiate your own and make it
active:

```ts
const camera = yield* Scene.instantiate("Camera", {});
yield* Scene.setCamera(camera);
```

## Moving it

The camera is an entity — `Motion.moveTo` works on it directly. On top of
that, `Camera` ships helpers for the moves that are *about the view*:

- `Camera.lookAt(point, duration?)` — set (or ease into) a **point of
  interest**. From then on the aim is pinned: however the camera's position
  moves, the target stays framed. No orientation math in the scene.
- `Camera.dolly`/`dollyTo(distance, duration, easing?)` — move along the
  **view axis**, toward/away from the point of interest. Not a z-tween
  (those only coincide while the camera is unrotated).
- `Camera.orbit`/`orbitTo(angle, duration, easing?)` — swing around the
  point of interest at constant distance; the turntable move.
- `Camera.follow(instance, options?)` — track a moving instance.

`lookAt` and `follow` name their target in the verb, so they have no
base/To pair; `dolly` and `orbit` animate a value and keep the full pair,
like any animator.

## HUD: opting out of the camera

A `Hud` entity's subtree is projected through the *identity* camera — it
stays bolted to the glass while the world moves behind it. Titles,
lower-thirds, and annotations go there.
